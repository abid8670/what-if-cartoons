# What If All Water Disappeared? — Script (3 min)

What if every drop of water on Earth simply vanished in an instant?
Not evaporated. Not frozen. Gone.

The first thing you would notice is the silence.
No waves crashing. No rain tapping on your window.
The oceans — all of them — would leave behind nothing but salt and sand.
The Great Barrier Reef, gone in a blink.

Within hours, the air would change.
Water vapor makes up about one percent of our atmosphere.
Without it, the sky would look sharper, drier, almost alien.
And the temperature? It would swing wildly.
Water is Earth's thermostat.
Without it, deserts would hit fifty degrees Celsius by day
and plunge below freezing at night.

But the real crisis starts inside your body.
You are about sixty percent water.
Your blood, your cells, your brain — all of it depends on H2O.
Without drinking, a human survives maybe three days.
After that, organs begin to shut down.

Plants would be next.
Crops would wither within days.
Forests would turn to kindling.
Wildfires would rage unchecked, because there is no water to fight them.

And then there is the geology.
Water shapes our planet.
Rivers carve canyons.
Glaciers grind mountains.
Without water, erosion stops.
The landscape would freeze in time — dry, cracked, and silent.

But here is the twist.
Earth has survived without surface water before.
Billions of years ago, our planet was a dry rock.
Water arrived from icy comets and volcanic outgassing.
So maybe, given enough time, water could return.
But for us, the humans, there would be no waiting.
We would be gone long before the first drop fell again.

So the real question is not whether Earth could survive.
It is whether we could.

What if the rules were different?
What if water was never here at all?
This is What If Theory.
